**Amazon Marketplace** is a third-party seller platform operated by Amazon that allows independent businesses and individuals to sell products directly to consumers on Amazon's website. As of 2025, the platform hosts approximately **1.9 million active sellers** worldwide and generates an estimated **$575 billion in annual gross merchandise value (GMV)** from third-party sales, representing more than 60% of all units sold on Amazon.

Amazon Marketplace was launched in November 2000 and has grown to become one of the largest commercial platforms in the world. The platform operates across multiple international marketplaces, including the United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, India, Canada, and Australia. As of 2025, 9.7 million seller accounts have been registered on the platform, though approximately 80% are dormant or semi-active.

The marketplace is characterized by a high concentration of Chinese sellers — who represent 59.9% of new seller registrations — and a dominant private label business model used by 67% of active sellers. Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA), launched in 2006, has been adopted by 82% of active sellers and is widely credited as the infrastructure that enabled the marketplace's scale.

Following a peak of 2.4 million active sellers in 2021, driven by pandemic-era e-commerce acceleration, the marketplace has entered a period of maturation. Active seller counts stabilized at 1.9 million by 2024–2025, while new daily registrations fell from approximately 4,000 in 2021 to 550 in 2025 — the lowest rate in a decade. Despite this contraction, the number of sellers generating $1 million or more in annual revenue grew from 60,000 in 2021 to 100,000 in 2025.

## History

Amazon launched its third-party marketplace in November 2000, initially as a way to allow other retailers to sell products alongside Amazon's own inventory. The program expanded significantly following the introduction of **Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA)** in 2006, which allowed third-party sellers to store inventory in Amazon's fulfillment centers and have Amazon handle shipping, returns, and customer service.

The marketplace grew steadily through the 2010s, accelerating as Amazon expanded internationally and as Chinese cross-border sellers began entering the platform in significant numbers beginning around 2015. The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020–2021 produced a surge in new seller registrations, with the platform reaching a peak of approximately **2.4 million active sellers** in 2021 and recording approximately **4,000 new seller registrations per day**.

Following the pandemic peak, the marketplace entered a period of normalization. Active seller counts declined from 2.4 million in 2021 to approximately 1.9 million in 2024–2025, while new daily registrations fell to approximately 550 per day — the lowest rate in a decade.

## Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA)

Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) is a service that allows third-party sellers to store products in Amazon's fulfillment centers. Amazon then handles picking, packing, shipping, returns, and customer service for FBA orders. Products fulfilled through FBA are eligible for Amazon Prime shipping.

As of 2025, **82% of active Amazon sellers** use FBA for at least part of their inventory. FBA adoption has been a key driver of the marketplace's growth, enabling sellers to offer Prime-eligible products without managing their own logistics infrastructure.

FBA adoption correlates with higher revenue outcomes. In the United States, where FBA infrastructure is most mature, 43% of active sellers achieve $100,000 or more in annual revenue, compared to a global average of 19%.

## Business Models

Amazon sellers use several primary business models:

| Business Model | % of Sellers | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Private Label | 67% | Sellers source products (often from manufacturers) and sell under a proprietary brand name |
| Wholesale / Retail Arbitrage | 23% | Sellers purchase branded products at wholesale or retail prices and resell on Amazon |
| Dropshipping | 10% | Sellers list products without holding inventory; suppliers ship directly to customers |

**Private label** is the dominant model, used by approximately two-thirds of Amazon sellers. Private label sellers typically source products from manufacturers — often in China — and sell them under their own brand names.

## Market Maturation

Following the 2021 peak, Amazon Marketplace has entered a period of maturation characterized by:

- **Declining new entrants**: Daily new registrations fell from ~4,000 in 2021 to ~550 in 2025
- **Stable active seller count**: Active sellers have stabilized at approximately 1.9 million
- **Growing high-revenue segment**: Million-dollar sellers grew 67% while total active sellers declined 21%
- **Increasing professionalization**: Higher barriers to entry have filtered out less-experienced operators
- **Revenue concentration**: A small percentage of sellers account for a disproportionate share of GMV

These trends are consistent with patterns observed in other maturing marketplace ecosystems, where initial growth phases characterized by broad participation give way to consolidation among more sophisticated operators.
